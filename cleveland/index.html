Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Cleveland

A little routine care is the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually encodes the month and year it was built, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that range and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can keep stressing the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
