Here is what shortens most water heaters' lives: corrosion from the inside and mineral buildup on the bottom, both of which are largely preventable. As water heats, dissolved minerals come out of the water and settle into a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On a gas unit, that layer sits between the flame and the water, so the steel runs hotter and the tank starts to rumble and pop. On an electric unit, it buries the lower element until the element burns out. Meanwhile the anode rod, a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not, dissolves a little every month. Once it is gone, the tank itself becomes the target. In Parma, OH, we see this pattern constantly, and most of it was preventable.

Each visit follows the same thorough checklist. The tank is drained and flushed to clear the sediment layer. The anode rod comes out for inspection and is replaced when little of it remains. The T&P relief valve is tested so we know it will open under pressure and close again cleanly. On gas models, we check the burner, pilot assembly, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue, and draft. Electric heaters get element and thermostat testing and a check of the wiring connections. We also measure the air charge in the expansion tank, check the water pressure, set the thermostat to a scald-safe level unless a mixing valve allows higher storage, and inspect the drain valve, connections, and pan.

Tankless owners need maintenance too, just a different kind. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers flow problems, fluctuating temperatures, and error codes. We descale the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A tankless unit showing error codes or fluctuating temperatures
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater
  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank as it heats
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap
  • Shorter showers before the water turns lukewarm
  • Higher gas or electric costs for the same routine

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
  • Stay on top of tankless descaling to avoid error codes and reduced flow
  • Replace a heavily consumed anode rod to extend the tank's life
  • Schedule a flush every year, or every six months if you have hard water
  • Check the drain pan and fittings every few months for drips or rust
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
