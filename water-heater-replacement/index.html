Warning Signs to Watch For

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • A heater older than most tanks last, usually eight to twelve years
  • Higher gas or electric bills for less hot water
  • Water leaking from the bottom or body of the tank
  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area
  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs
  • Discolored hot water, a sign the tank is corroding inside
  • Repairs that keep coming back, one part after another

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Replacement

A water heater swap seems easy right up until you count the connections. Gas units need a leak-free connection with a sediment trap, a correctly sized and pitched vent that sends carbon monoxide outdoors, and enough combustion air. An electric unit needs a circuit and breaker matched to the element wattage. Every tank needs a correctly rated relief valve with a discharge pipe routed to a safe spot, and many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps under local code. Going tankless or switching to a heat pump adds even more: bigger gas lines, different vent materials, dedicated circuits, and condensate drains.

What you get from a pro is a replacement that is done once. The new heater is matched to your demand, the gas supply, venting, and panel are confirmed, and the permit is handled if your jurisdiction requires one. We route the relief valve discharge correctly, add the code items that apply, test for leaks and proper draft, and take the old tank with us. Rushed swaps tend to leave missing expansion tanks, bad vent pitch, and undersized gas lines behind. Ours are done to code and to last. And if a simple repair is the better call for your heater, we say so, without pressure.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Write the install date on the tank so you know when to plan the next replacement
  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
  • Have water pressure checked; above about 80 psi, a pressure reducing valve protects the new heater
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear

Should You DIY or Call a Pro?

A few tasks, like checking the temperature setting or lifting the relief valve lever to test it, are fine for homeowners, but most water heater work involves gas lines, venting, 240-volt circuits, or a tank full of scalding water, and that belongs with a professional.
