Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Violent rumbling or banging from the heater as it runs
  • A complete loss of hot water throughout the house
  • Water or steam coming from the temperature and pressure relief valve or its discharge pipe
  • A heater breaker that will not stay on after being reset
  • The smell of gas near the unit, a situation that calls for evacuating and phoning the utility or 911
  • Tap water hot enough to burn, even at the usual setting
  • Black soot or heat discoloration around the burner door or draft hood
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater or spreading across the floor

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

The risks in a water heater emergency are easy to underestimate. In reality, you may be dealing with standing water beside 240-volt wiring, a gas supply and open burner a few inches from a puddle, and a tank of very hot water under pressure. The relief valve, if it is firing, is the final line of defense between an overheated tank and a violent failure, and plugging it is one of the most dangerous things anyone can do to a heater. Relighting a pilot while gas lingers, or cranking on a corroded fitting until it snaps, can make things much worse. Leave, shut off, and call is the right sequence for anyone without the tools and training.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and the right parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution instead of relying on a sniff test, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Equally important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a modest repair bill versus the cost of a whole new unit. You get that answer before any work begins.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Ask about a drain pan and drain line, especially for heaters in attics, closets, or above finished rooms
  • Drain a few gallons or flush the tank annually to keep sediment from cooking the tank floor
  • Keep the thermostat near 120F to limit scalding and reduce strain on the tank
  • Know where the heater shutoff and the main water valve are, and make sure everyone in the house does too
  • Store chemicals and flammables far from the burner, and keep the area around the heater uncluttered
  • Add a battery leak alarm or an automatic shutoff valve that closes the supply when it senses water
  • Lift the relief valve lever once a year to confirm it opens and reseats, and call if it keeps dripping
