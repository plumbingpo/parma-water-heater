Keeping Your Strongsville Water Heater Running Longer

Many premature tank failures trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms such as discolored hot water, rumbling during heating, dampness under the tank, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a flooded utility room.
