How Garfield Heights Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Most premature tank failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Drain and flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as rusty hot water, rumbling during heating, dampness under the tank, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one with water on the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both modest fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can keep stressing the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
